\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conc}

This thesis introduced the incremental temporal action segmentation task in an endeavor to push TAS research into developing approaches for models with continual learning capabilities. We proposed a temporally coherent action model for incremental procedural video understanding from the perspective of video data replay. Departing from the conventional frame-exemplar data replay prevalent in action recognition, our approach adopts a generative model. In addition, we facilitate the modeling of temporal coherence in the actions by introducing a conditioning variable to the generative model. Our design guarantees both feature diversity and temporal coherence in the replay data, resulting in a significant performance improvement on three action segmentation benchmarks compared to the baselines. 

\section{Limitations}

This work presupposes the presence of dense labels for all videos within each incremental task, and our TCA model relies on such dense labels for effective learning. However, such an annotation process can be expensive in a real-world scenario. Additionally, while we can demonstrate the temporal coherence of the features generated by our TCA model, a qualitative evaluation of the features remains challenging because their existence in a feature space makes them difficult to decode into a human-readable format.

\section{Future Work}

Much research into weaker forms of supervision in TAS has been conducted, given the significant costs involved in curating a fully temporally annotated dataset. Weaker forms of supervision such as action transcripts~\cite{kuehne2017weakly} and timestamp supervision~\cite{li2021temporal,rahaman2022generalized} have achieved great results in recent years. Exploring the iTAS setup with weak supervision would be a promising direction for future research.

We hypothesize that due to the frame imbalance between tasks, tasks with longer videos (e.g. task 5) can dominate the training set and negatively affect model performance in other tasks, especially if encountered early in the sequence. A more considered approach to action sequence sampling or replay memory management can be explored in future work to alleviate this phenomenon.

Temporal action segmentation is an exciting and rapidly advancing field. We must progress towards developing models with incremental learning capabilities to reach adoption into real-world systems. We hope our work will spur others in the field to consider continual learning when designing algorithms for TAS.
